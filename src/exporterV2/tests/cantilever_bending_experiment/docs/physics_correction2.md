Corrections/additions I'd make to the document's plan

1. Watch beam slenderness at high N in the convergence test (Section 10.1). This is a real gap. Euler-Bernoulli theory itself — not just the discretization — assumes length ≫ cross-section diameter; it "is strictly valid for long, slender" beams and does not account for shear deformation, and "should not be applied to thick or deep beams" since it underestimates deflection once shear becomes non-negligible. Concretely: the document's own N=40 test on a 0.20 m branch gives a 5 mm link length against an ~11 mm stalk diameter — a segment shorter than it is wide. At that point EB-derived kθ = EI/ℓ is no longer a good local model regardless of how correctly it's implemented, and your convergence curve may stop converging to the "expected" analytical EB deflection not because of a bug but because the physical beam theory itself is being pushed past its validity range. If N=20–40 convergence tests show a small but persistent divergence from the analytical EB result, that's a signal to check slenderness before hunting for a code bug. If needed later, a Timoshenko correction (shear term added to compliance) is the standard fix, but I wouldn't add that complexity unless the convergence tests actually show it's needed.

2. Explicitly test that fixing the two bugs together doesn't mask each other. The metersPerUnit bug (100× length error) and the missing EI-scaling (which the document shows goes the other direction — more joints without rescaling makes the represented EI smaller, i.e. softer, not harder) don't obviously combine to produce "10 joints feels stiffer than 3." That means the observed stiffening is most likely dominated by the confounded geometry/mass in the experimental script (which the document does flag) or by solver iteration effects on the long chain — not by the units bug alone. Worth running the corrected 3-vs-10 test with metersPerUnit fixed and iteration count held constant and EI-scaling applied, all three at once, so you can attribute the pre/post difference correctly instead of fixing three things and only checking the final aggregate.

Minor: the compliance-in-series formula for adjacent segments (Section 3.4) implicitly assumes the bending moment is continuous across the joint (same physical assumption as springs in series carrying the same load). That's the standard and correct assumption for a beam under pure bending with no point moment applied exactly at the joint — fine for your case, just worth knowing it's an assumption rather than a general identity, in case you ever add concentrated loads (e.g., fruit clusters) exactly at joint locations.

Nothing else in the document needs correcting — the unit conversion, the EI-based stiffness derivation, the fixed-length discretization test design, and the "damping should not compensate for a wrong static stiffness" ordering are all standard, correctly-motivated practice for this kind of reduced-order plant model.
